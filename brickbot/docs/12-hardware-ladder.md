# 12 — The hardware ladder

[← Mortar research](11-mortar-research.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Next: Troubleshooting →](13-troubleshooting.md)

| Arm | Payload | Price | Brick capability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SO-101** (you are here) | 0.2–0.5 kg | $120–500 | Mini bricks; half-scale clay at the limit |
| Koch v1.1 | sub-kg | ~$250/arm | Sideways move — skip |
| Trossen WidowX-250 / ViperX-300 | 0.25 / 0.75 kg | $3.1k / $4.8k | Weak value next to PiPER — skip |
| **AgileX PiPER** ⭐ | 1.5 kg | ~$2,499 | AAC brick-size units (~1 kg); 0.1 mm repeatability; LeRobot + ROS2 support |
| ARX R5/X5 | ~1.5 kg | ~$5–10k | Research favorite (Stanford UMI lineage), same class as PiPER |
| **Unitree Z1 Pro** | 4 kg | ~$15k | **First arm that lifts a standard clay brick with margin**; joint torque sensors |
| UR5e / UR10e | 5 / 12.5 kg | $30–60k | Industrial cobot grade; what SAM100 built on |

Notes:

- At the Z1/UR tier, budget another **$1–5k for a wide parallel or vacuum-assist gripper** — brick geometry demands one.
- Rule of thumb: real-brick-capable arm = **$15k floor** (Z1), **$30k+** for cobot-grade reliability.
- The **PiPER is the recommended Stage-4 buy**: the best price-per-kg jump from the SO-101, and enough for AAC-format real masonry.
